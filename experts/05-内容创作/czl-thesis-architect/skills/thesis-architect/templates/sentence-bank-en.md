# English Academic Sentence Bank

> Borrow the structure, not the words. Swap in your own content.

## 1. Opening / framing

1. Despite growing interest in X, little is known about Y.
2. Prior work has established X; far less clear is Y.
3. Participation in X varies widely across Y, yet existing evidence comes mostly from Z.
4. This study examines how X shapes Y in the context of Z.
5. Two competing accounts of X have emerged over the past decade.

## 2. Stating the research question

6. This study asks whether X is associated with Y, and whether Z explains that association.
7. Specifically, we examine three questions: (i) …, (ii) …, and (iii) ….
8. Unlike prior work, we treat X as an explanatory rather than a control variable.

## 3. Presenting disagreement

9. Studies supporting this view report … (L1, L2), whereas others find … (L3, L4).
10. The discrepancy may stem from differences in sampling, measurement, or model specification.
11. Notably, the two studies reporting opposite results analysed the same dataset; the divergence lies in how the outcome was constructed.

## 4. Evaluating literature

12. The main contribution of that work is …, although its sample covers only … .
13. The finding holds under … but may not generalise to … .
14. These studies share a common limitation: none addresses … .

## 5. Reporting results

15. Participation ranged from 48.7% to 82.1% across the three schools, a gap of 33 percentage points.
16. The difference was significant (F(2, 309) = 24.7, p < .001, η² = 0.14).
17. Commuting time was negatively associated with weekly participation (r = −0.38, p < .001, 95% CI [−0.47, −0.28]).
18. After controlling for household income, the coefficient on commuting time remained −0.24 (SE = 0.05, p < .001), while income was not significant (β = 0.06, p = .31).
19. The pattern held across all three subsamples (Appendix Table A1).
20. Given the small sample, this estimate is unstable and is reported descriptively only.

## 6. Interpreting

21. One interpretation is that … .
22. This pattern is consistent with the view that … .
23. A testable implication follows: if X operates through Y, we should observe … in … .
24. The effect appears to run not through X but through Y.

## 7. Handling alternative explanations

25. An alternative account—that …—receives little support: … .
26. Our data cannot distinguish between these two explanations; doing so requires … .
27. This alternative may partly hold, but it cannot explain why … .

## 8. Stating limitations

28. Three limitations qualify these findings.
29. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference: reverse causality cannot be ruled out.
30. The sample is confined to three county schools; generalisation to urban settings should be made with caution.
31. Participation was self-reported and may overstate actual attendance.

## 9. Concluding

32. These findings suggest that … .
33. We do not claim that …; we claim only that … .
34. The conclusion holds under … ; beyond that condition, it awaits testing.
35. In practice, the binding constraint appears to be … rather than … .
36. Future work should track the same students over time to identify causal direction.

## 10. Citing

37. Prior research has shown … (Chen, 2022).
38. Chen (2022) found … in two county schools, although the sample was small.
39. This claim traces back to … (L5, p. 47).
40. Contrary to …, we argue that … .

## 11. Referring to figures

41. Table 4-1 reports descriptive statistics for the three schools.
42. As Figure 4-1 shows, participation declines steadily from urban to remote schools.
43. Figure 4-2 compares participation under the two definitions; the largest gap reaches 21 percentage points.

## 12. Transitions

44. That said, this evidence is indirect.
45. More importantly, the pattern holds across specifications.
46. Having established X, we now turn to Y.
47. The next section explains how this variation arises.

## 13. Hedging ladder

| Strength | Verbs | When |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | demonstrate, establish, show | multiple sources, causal design |
| Moderate | suggest, indicate, point to | single source, association |
| Weak | may, might, appears to, is likely to | indirect, small sample |

## 14. Do not use

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| It is worth noting that | (delete, state the point) |
| With the development of society | Since 2021, … |
| plays an important role in | specify the mechanism |
| obviously / clearly / of course | (delete) |
| more and more researchers | twelve studies since 2018 (L1–L12) |
| further research is needed | future work should track … over time |
| proves / confirms | suggests / is consistent with |
